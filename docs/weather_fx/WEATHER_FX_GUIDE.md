# Weather tile effects: guide

Brief for Claude Design and for a developer. Goal: replace the current look of the 8
"Погода" tiles (`docs/claude_design_briefs/light_tiles_canvas/renders/Glass_Weather.png`,
generated by `build/gen_glass.py`, functions `wx_kind` … `wx_art`) with effects that read
as weather photographs, not icons.

Working reference: `weather_demo.html` in this folder (one self-contained file, Canvas 2D,
no WebGL, no external files). Published copy: https://claude.ai/artifact/7SsKp118DF2Wuj3jCisSba
(private until shared from the page's Share menu). Screenshots: `shots/`.

## 1. What is wrong now (visual reading of the render, not measured)

| Tile | Defect | Root cause |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | flat disc, identical straight rays | no luminance falloff; rays equal in length, width and alpha |
| Clouds | blurred bands of one grey | single tone; no light direction, so no lit edge and no darker base |
| Rain | uniform thin dashes; glass drops are dots | one depth layer; drops have no lens behaviour |
| Snow vs night | both are white dots | same primitive; no depth blur, no drift, no sky colour difference |
| Frost | "+" glyphs | a symbol, not a growth pattern |
| Lightning | wavy vertical line | no branching, no glow, no flash of the cloud |
| All | near-black palette | weather is not readable by colour at a glance |

## 2. Rules for all tiles

**Layout stays.** Glass card (radius 24, 1 px inner edge highlight, soft drop shadow),
text on top: header (icon + «Погода»), temperature, word, `↓ min ↑ max`. All text is white
with a soft shadow (`0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,.3), 0 0 14px rgba(0,0,0,.14)`), because the sky
under it changes.

**Each state has its own sky gradient** (top → bottom; values used in the demo):

| State | Top | Bottom | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear day | `#1b63c2` | `#8fc6f2` | warm glow around the sun, deeper blue in the far corner |
| Clear night | `#040817` | `#24365f` | faint warm light-pollution glow at the horizon |
| Partly cloudy | `#2a6cb8` | `#8db8dc` | glow around the sun |
| Overcast | `#56626d` | `#8a959f` | — |
| Rain | `#2b3644` | `#5a6a7f` | light mist band in the lower 40 % |
| Thunderstorm | `#17161f` | `#433e56` | purple bias |
| Snow | `#46576c` | `#8796a7` | — |
| Frost (clear, cold) | `#1f5a98` | `#7fb0da` | cold white glow around a low sun |

The sky colour is the first cue; the effect is the second. The tiles keep their sky
colours on both light and dark pages (as Apple Weather widgets do); only the page
background, captions and card shadow change with the theme.

**Rendering budget (hard rule).**
- Up to 8 tiles can be on screen at once on an iPhone PWA. Assume all 8 animate.
- Baseline is Canvas 2D (or SVG) per tile. WebGL is optional and may run on **one
  tile at a time, only while it is visible**, with a Canvas 2D fallback that looks
  acceptable on its own. Never one WebGL context per tile (Safari limits contexts and
  drops the oldest).
- Pause every tile outside the viewport (`IntersectionObserver`) and when the page is
  hidden (`visibilitychange`).
- Cap frame rate per effect: 30 fps for slow motion (sun, stars, clouds, frost), 60 fps
  only for fast particles (rain, snow).
- Cap canvas pixel density at 2 (`min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`); iPhone is 3, the gain is
  not visible on soft effects and costs 2.25× pixels.
- Do expensive work once: noise fields, sprites, moon, bolt shapes are pre-rendered into
  offscreen canvases at setup; per frame you only move and composite them.
- Do not use `ctx.filter`, `shadowBlur` or CSS `filter: blur()` per frame. For blur,
  pre-render a soft sprite, or downscale and upscale once.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: draw one still frame per tile, no loop, no
  lightning flashes. The still frame must still read as the weather (stars visible,
  frost fully grown, rain streaks frozen mid-air).
- Lightning flashes: at most 2 bright pulses per strike, at least 5 s between strikes,
  flash overlay alpha ≤ 0.15 of the tile (WCAG 2.3.1 sets a limit of 3 flashes per second).
- Add a frame-time governor: if the mean frame interval over 30 frames is above 24 ms,
  every tile halves (then thirds) its frame rate; restore when it drops under 18 ms.

## 3. Effects

Each effect: what it should look like → technique → parameters (CSS px at tile size
≈ 175 × 126 pt) → cost per tile → what to avoid.

### 3.1 Sun (clear day; also behind clouds and the cold winter sun)

**Look.** The sun is the brightest thing in the frame and is overexposed: a white core
with no visible edge, a warm halo that fades smoothly over most of the tile, and faint
streaks of glare of different lengths. The sky around it is lighter and warmer
(forward scattering); the far corner is deeper blue. Optional: 2–3 faint lens ghosts on
the line from the sun through the tile centre.

**Technique.** Pre-render three sprites once:
1. Bloom: radial gradient, radius R ≈ 1.4 H, stops (as fraction of R):
   `0` white `1.0` · `0.9·q` `1.0` · `1.3·q` `.78` · `2.6·q` `.34` · `0.3` `.13` · `0.62` `.04` · `1` `0`,
   where q = disc radius / R, disc radius ≈ 0.075 H. Warm tint (255,240,200 → 255,205,145);
   cold sun: white-blue and R ≈ 1.05 H.
2. Rays: 16–26 thin wedges, each with its own length (0.35–1.0 R), half-width
   (0.3°–2°) and alpha (0.03–0.10), filled with a radial fade.
3. Ghosts: soft discs, radius 3–12 px, alpha 0.05–0.09, tinted green / violet / amber.

Per frame: draw bloom with `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'`, scale breathing ±2 %
over 6 s; draw the ray sprite rotating +0.7°/s and a second copy, 80 % size, rotating
−1.1°/s at 55 % alpha. Two counter-rotating sets give a shimmer that never repeats.

Sun behind clouds: sample cloud density at the sun position from the cloud layers'
density arrays (no pixel readback), intensity `k = 0.25 + 0.75·Π(1 − coverageᵢ)`; draw the
bloom under the clouds at `k` and a smaller copy (0.6 R) over them at `0.35·k` (lens glare
over cloud edges).

**Cost.** 3–4 `drawImage` per frame. Demo: 0.19 ms JS per draw (§6).

**Avoid.** A hard-edged disc; equal rays; ray rotation faster than ~2°/s (reads as a
spinner); `shadowBlur` for glow.

### 3.2 Moon and stars (clear night)

**Look.** A small moon with a real phase (terminator follows a sphere), darker maria,
a faint cold halo; the unlit part is barely darker than the sky. Stars: many faint,
few bright; slightly different colours (blue-white, white, warm); bright ones with a
tiny 4-point diffraction cross; twinkle stronger near the horizon; fade into the
horizon glow. Stars do not move. That plus the dark navy sky separates them from snow.

**Technique.**
- Moon sprite once, per pixel: normal `n = (x, y, √(1−x²−y²))`, light
  `L = (sin α, 0, cos α)` with `α = acos(2k − 1)` for lit fraction k; `lit =
  smoothstep(−0.05, 0.12, n·L)`; albedo `0.95 − 0.22·maria(fbm) + 0.05·craters(fbm)`;
  brightness `albedo·(0.84 + 0.16·n_z)`. Radius ≈ 0.13 H. Halo: radial gradient to 9 r,
  alpha 0.22 → 0.
- Stars: three layers, counts 70 / 22 / 5, radius 0.3–0.55 / 0.6–0.9 / 1.0–1.35 px,
  alpha 0.25–0.55 / 0.5–0.85 / 0.85–1. Twinkle: `alpha·(1 − amp·(½ + ½ sin(2πt/period + φ)))`,
  `amp = 0.15 + 0.55·(y/H)`, period 1.4–5 s. Fade `× (1 − 0.8·smoothstep(0.5, 0.95, y/H))`.
  Draw each star as a pre-rendered soft dot sprite.
- Optional: one thin, slow (2 px/s), 40 %-alpha cloud wisp lit cold blue from the moon side.

**Cost.** ~100 small `drawImage` per frame at 30 fps. Demo: 0.26 ms JS per draw;
setup 28 ms (moon sprite + wisp layer).

**Avoid.** A crescent glyph; all stars same size and colour; twinkling by
on/off blinking; moving stars.

### 3.3 Clouds (three coverage levels)

**Look.** Clouds are lit from one side: bright edge toward the sun, grey-blue shadow on
the far side and at the base. Cumulus have rounded tops and flatter, darker bases.
Overcast is continuous and stretched horizontally with darker and lighter patches.
Depth comes from 2–3 layers that move at different speeds (parallax): far layer slow,
lighter and more transparent; near layer faster, darker, larger features.

**Technique (Canvas 2D, used in the demo).** For each layer, once at setup, compute a
sprite 2 W wide and H high:
1. Density field: fractional Brownian motion of value noise, 5 octaves, **periodic in x**
   so the sprite tiles seamlessly; offset each octave by a non-integer shift to hide the
   lattice grid; **domain-warp** the coordinates with two 3-octave noises (warp ≈ 0.9 cell)
   to get billows instead of blocks.
2. Vertical mask per layer: fade in at `top`, cut at `base` (sharp cut = flat cumulus base).
3. Coverage: `D = clamp((n·mask − threshold) / soft, 0, 1)`.
4. Lighting: sample the raw field a few px toward the light (`(+5, −6)` px for a sun in the
   upper right) and use the difference: `L = 0.55 + edge·(R − R_towardLight) +
   vgain·(vertical − ½) − dark·D`. Colour = mix(shade, lit, L). Use the raw (unclamped)
   field for the difference, otherwise the inside of a cloud goes flat and the edges get
   contour lines.
5. Write RGBA into `ImageData`, `putImageData` once.

Per frame: draw each layer twice (offset and offset + 2 W) — 2 `drawImage` per layer.

| Level | Layers | threshold / soft | Speeds px/s | Lit / shade colours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Partly cloudy | 3, cells 4/3/2, base 0.5/0.72/1.0 | 0.50–0.55 / 0.12–0.16 | 1.8 / 3.2 / 5.5 | white / `#768aa6` |
| Overcast | 3, stretch 1.6–2.2 | 0.30–0.47 / 0.18–0.25 | 1.2 / 2.4 / 4 | `#cbd1da` / `#4e5764` |
| Rain / storm | 3, near layer low and dark | 0.30–0.50 / 0.12–0.25 | 3/5/8 (storm × 1.7) | `#7c8896` / `#242a34`; storm purple-black |

Generate at CSS resolution, capped at 220 px of tile width (`res = min(1, 220/W)`);
clouds are soft, and this keeps setup bounded on wide tiles.

**SVG alternative.** `feTurbulence` (type `fractalNoise`, baseFrequency ≈ 0.008–0.02,
4–5 octaves) + `feColorMatrix` to threshold alpha + `feDiffuseLighting` with a
`feDistantLight` for the lit edge (CSS-Tricks article in §5). It looks good in a static
render, but animating `feTurbulence` re-runs the filter per frame and is expensive on
WebKit. If you use SVG, render once and animate only `transform: translateX` of the result.

**Optional WebGL.** Raymarched fbm clouds (volumetric-clouds repo) look best and are too
heavy for 8 tiles; for one hero view only.

**Cost.** Per frame 6 `drawImage` (3 layers × 2). Demo JS: 0.02–0.08 ms per draw.
Setup: 38–54 ms per tile (three layers) in Chromium on this container.

**Avoid.** Single-tone blur; horizontal speed above ~10 px/s (reads as a timelapse);
the same noise seed for every layer; animating the filter itself.

### 3.4 Rain in the air

**Look.** Long thin streaks, slightly slanted by wind, in three depths: far = thin, short,
faint and slower on screen; near = few, longer, brighter. Streaks are lighter than the
cloud behind them.

**Technique.** Particles in three arrays; one `beginPath` + `stroke` per layer.

| Layer | Count rain / storm | Width px | Length px | Speed px/s | Alpha |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Far | 70 / 130 | 0.6 | 7–11 | 380–480 | 0.22 (+0.05 storm) |
| Mid | 34 / 60 | 0.9 | 13–19 | 620–760 | 0.32 |
| Near | 9 / 16 | 1.3 | 24–34 | 950–1150 | 0.42 |

Slant: `dx/dy = 0.17` (rain), `0.30` (storm); storm speeds × 1.15. Recycle a drop above the
tile when it leaves the bottom.

**Cost.** 3 strokes per frame regardless of count. Demo (with glass drops): 0.31 ms JS per draw at 60 fps.

**Avoid.** Equal dashes; vertical rain with zero wind; drawing each drop as its own path.

### 3.5 Rain on glass, with refraction

**Look.** Small static beads everywhere; a few larger drops. Each larger drop is a lens:
it shows the scene behind it **upside down and mirrored**, compressed, with a dark rim
(strongest at the bottom), a sharp specular highlight upper-left and a faint caustic at
the bottom. Larger drops occasionally slide down, wobble, stop, leave a trail of tiny
beads that dry out in 2.5–5 s, and swallow beads in their path (growing).

**Technique (Canvas 2D baseline).**
- Keep a "scene" canvas: sky + clouds composited; refresh it once per second (clouds move slowly).
- Small beads (0.5–1.7 px radius, cap 60 rain / 70 storm, spawn 5–8 per second): draw a
  pre-rendered drop sprite (transparent centre, dark rim, highlight). No refraction needed.
- Big drops (5–6, radius 2.2–5.5 px): `save` → clip to an ellipse → `translate` to the drop,
  `scale(−1, −1)` → `drawImage(scene, region 8 r wide around the drop → drop box)` →
  `restore` → draw the rim/highlight sprite. Elongate vertically up to +28 % while sliding.
- Sliding: hold 0.5–5 s, then accelerate to 25–80 px/s (storm 110), stop with probability
  0.35/s; merge: `r = ∛(r³ + r_bead³)`, cap 5.5 px; respawn at the top after leaving.
- Clamp the source rectangle inside the scene canvas (Safari draws nothing for an
  out-of-bounds source rect).

**Optional WebGL path (one visible tile only).** Draw drop shapes into a small canvas
(red/green = normal offset, alpha = thickness) on the CPU, upload it as a texture, and in
a fragment shader offset the lookup into a blurred background texture by the normal
(refraction), plus a highlight from the normal. That is the codrops RainEffect idea and
the Atmos FX 0.4 architecture (§5). Fall back to the 2D path when WebGL is unavailable
or the tile scrolls out. Not built in the demo.

**Cost.** ~70 sprite draws + 5–6 clipped draws per frame. Demo: rain tile 0.31 ms,
storm 0.30 ms JS per draw at 60 fps.

**Avoid.** Dots without a rim; refraction of a static picture that does not match the sky
colour; per-frame blur filters; more than ~10 clipped drops per tile.

### 3.6 Lightning (thunderstorm)

**Look.** A branching, jagged channel from the cloud base down, white core with a blue
glow, partly hidden by the nearest cloud layer. The cloud lights up from inside around
the top of the bolt. Timing: two quick pulses and a short afterglow, then darkness for
5–10 s.

**Technique.**
- Bolt shape: midpoint displacement from (x0, 0.12 H) to (x0 ± 0.25 W, 0.85–1.05 H),
  7 levels, displacement starts at 0.3 W and × 0.55 each level; 2–4 branches from the
  first 70 % of the channel at 25°–50° off vertical, length 0.2–0.4 H, 5 levels.
  Render once per strike into an offscreen canvas with three strokes per path
  (7 px rgba(160,170,255,.16); 2.6 px rgba(205,212,255,.55); 1.1 px white), branches at
  half width.
- Envelope: pulses at 0 ms (amp 1, decay τ 30 ms), 140 ms (0.8, 30 ms) and an afterglow
  at 200 ms (0.45, 160 ms); 15 ms linear rise each.
- Draw order: sky → far + mid cloud → in-cloud glow (radial 0.6 W at the bolt top,
  `lighter`) → bolt → near cloud (hides the bolt top) → rain → full-tile overlay
  `rgba(190,198,255, 0.14·e)` → glass drops.
- Reduced motion: no strikes at all.

**Cost.** One `drawImage` + one radial fill during a strike; bolt generation < 1 ms (not
separately measured). Demo storm tile: 0.30 ms JS per draw.

**Avoid.** A single wavy line; flashing the whole tile to white; more than 2 bright pulses;
periodic timing.

### 3.7 Snow

**Look.** Grey-blue sky, a light high cloud layer. Flakes in three depths: far = small,
sharp, slow; mid = medium; near = few large out-of-focus discs (bokeh), faster. Flakes
sway sideways and drift with a light wind. A thin band of settled snow on the lower edge
of the glass.

**Technique.** Three pre-rendered radial sprites (sharp, soft, bokeh-flat).

| Layer | Count | Radius px | Fall px/s | Sway amplitude px | Sway freq Hz | Alpha |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Far | 80 | 0.7–1.3 | 10–18 | 3–8 | 0.25–0.6 | 0.6–0.85 |
| Mid | 34 | 1.6–2.7 | 22–34 | 6–14 | 0.25–0.6 | 0.85–1 |
| Near | 5 | 4.5–8 | 40–60 | 10–20 | 0.25–0.6 | 0.35–0.55 |

Wind +6 px/s scaled by depth. Settled snow: one static sprite, a noise height profile
2–11 px with a slight blue shadow above, built at setup.

**Cost.** ~120 `drawImage` per frame at 60 fps. Demo: 0.27 ms JS per draw.

**Avoid.** Same-size dots; straight vertical fall; snow on a near-black sky (it then
reads as stars).

### 3.8 Frost (clear and cold)

**Look.** Ice grows on the glass from the edges inward: dense in the corners, thinner
along the sides, the centre clear so the text reads. Feathery crystals with side branches
at about 60° (hexagonal ice), plus a white haze near the edges. A few sparkles flash on
crystals, briefly and rarely. The sky behind is a clear, cold blue with a low white sun.

**Technique.**
- Haze: at half resolution, `alpha = 0.62 · f · (0.4 + 0.6·fbm)`, where
  `f = (1 − d_edge/0.2H)^1.6 + 0.8·(1 − d_corner/0.6H)²`; reduce to 60 % in the text area
  (lower-left half).
- Crystals: seeds every ~9 px of the perimeter + 7 per corner; each grows inward in
  1.5 px steps with ±0.15 rad jitter; every 2–5 steps a side branch at ±60° (alternating
  sides) with 50 % (then 38 %) of the remaining length, up to depth 3. Reach 0.16 H
  × 0.5–1.15, × up to 2.6 near corners, × 0.6 in the text area. Line widths 1/0.75/0.55/0.4 px,
  alpha 0.55/0.42/0.30/0.22 by depth. Demo: 6341 segments.
- Growth: sort segments by distance from their seed; reveal at 24 px/s into an
  offscreen canvas (only new segments per frame, one `Path2D` per depth) — the frost
  grows in ~3–4 s when the tile first appears, then costs one `drawImage`.
- Sparkles: 16 points on depth-0/1 crystals; each lights for 8 % of a 3–7 s period
  (a sine bump), 5–9 px cross-shaped sprite, `lighter`.

**Cost.** During growth up to a few hundred segment strokes per frame; afterwards
3 `drawImage` + ≤16 sparkles. Demo: 0.23 ms mean, 1.3 ms p95 JS per draw (p95 = growth phase).

**Avoid.** Symbols ("+", "❄"); frost covering the temperature; constant glitter.

## 4. How the demo is built

- `TILES` (data) → `SCENES[kind](W, H, dpr, rng, seed)` returns `{ step(dt, t), draw(ctx, t), count() }`.
- One `requestAnimationFrame` loop for all tiles; each tile has its own clock, so a
  paused tile resumes without a jump; per-tile frame-rate cap (`FPS`), governor `slow`.
- `IntersectionObserver` (rootMargin 60 px) marks visible tiles; the loop stops when none is visible.
- `ResizeObserver` rebuilds a tile (debounced 150 ms) when its size changes.
- Seeded random generator per tile: the same tile looks the same on every load.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` or the «Движение: Выключено» toggle: one still frame at t = 7 s.
- «Замерить 5 с» button: forces all 8 tiles to animate for 5 s and prints a table
  (frame intervals, per-tile draw time, setup time, element count). Use it on the iPhone.
- `window.wxLab` exposes `measure(ms, only)`, `strike(hold)`, `setMotion(v)` for scripts.
- File is written for the claude.ai artifact skeleton (no `<html>/<head>/<body>`); to
  open locally with the same rendering, wrap it in `<!doctype html>` (the screenshot
  script does).

Porting to `gen_glass.py`: the generator outputs static HTML; the cheapest port is to
keep the tile markup and add one `<canvas>` per weather tile plus this script (≈ 48 KB
unminified). Porting to Swift later: the same layers map to `Canvas`/`TimelineView` in
SwiftUI or `CAEmitterLayer` for rain/snow; noise sprites can be pre-baked PNGs.

## 6. Measurements (2026-10-29)

**Environment.** Headless Chromium 153 (Playwright) on a Linux cloud container, **no
GPU** (software rasterisation). Phone emulation 393 × 852 pt, `deviceScaleFactor` 2,
tile size 168 × 120 pt, canvas 336 × 240 px. Script: `measure()` inside the page
(`performance.now()` around each tile's step + draw; `requestAnimationFrame` timestamps
for frame intervals). **Not an iPhone. No Safari/WebKit run.**

**What "JS ms per draw" means.** Time to issue the canvas commands. Chromium rasterises
canvases later, outside that interval, so rasterisation and compositing are **not** in
the per-tile numbers; they show up only in the frame interval.

### 6.1 All 8 tiles animating together, 5 s

| Tile | Cap fps | Draws/s | JS ms mean | p95 | max | Setup ms | Animated elements |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clear-day | 30 | 30.0 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 2.60 | 3.5 | 6 (bloom, 2 ray sets, 3 ghosts) |
| clear-night | 30 | 30.0 | 0.23 | 0.40 | 0.40 | 27.8 | 99 (97 stars, moon, 1 cloud layer) |
| partly | 30 | 30.0 | 0.08 | 0.20 | 0.60 | 37.9 | 4 (3 cloud layers, sun) |
| overcast | 30 | 30.0 | 0.02 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 54.2 | 3 (cloud layers) |
| rain | 60 | 59.6 | 0.30 | 0.50 | 3.00 | 45.3 | 181 (113 streaks, ~65 glass drops, 3 layers) |
| storm | 60 | 59.6 | 0.26 | 0.50 | 1.50 | 41.6 | 284 (206 streaks, ~74 drops, 3 layers, bolt) |
| snow | 60 | 59.6 | 0.26 | 0.40 | 1.90 | 24.8 | 121 (119 flakes, 2 layers) |
| frost | 30 | 30.0 | 0.22 | 1.10 | 2.10 | 17.6 | 19 (sun, 16 sparkles, haze, crystal canvas; 6341 segments drawn once) |

Frame interval (CPU not throttled): 298 frames in 5 s, mean 16.8 ms, p95 16.8 ms,
max 33.3 ms; 2 frames over 20 ms, 0 over 33.4 ms. JS for all 8 tiles together:
71 ms per second of wall time. Setup values come from an earlier run of the same page
before the governor was added (setup code unchanged).

### 6.2 Same with CPU throttled 4× (rough proxy for a slow phone, not calibrated to any iPhone)

| Scenario | Frames in 5 s | Interval mean | p95 | max | Governor level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 8 tiles forced | 47 | 109.1 ms | 150.0 ms | 216.7 ms | 3 (fps ÷ 3) |
| Only visible tiles (4 of 8 at load) | 171 | 29.4 ms | 66.6 ms | 83.4 ms | 3 |
| One tile alone (each of 8, 3 s each) | — | 16.7–19.7 ms | 16.7–33.4 ms | — | — |

Per-tile JS at 4×: 0.13 ms (overcast) to 2.9 ms (frost) mean, p95 up to 9.3 ms (rain, storm).

**Reading.** Each tile alone is cheap. With 8 canvases updating together under 4×
throttling the frame interval collapses to ~9 fps, while the measured JS is only
~107 ms per second, so the bottleneck here is rasterising and compositing eight
336 × 240 canvases in software, not the effect code. The governor lowers draw rate but
does not fix that. Whether an iPhone (GPU-backed canvas in WebKit) has the same limit is
**not measured**. Mitigations if the phone run shows the same: draw all tiles into one
shared canvas behind the grid; drop to 30 fps for rain/snow; DPR cap 1.5.

### 6.3 Behaviour checks
